# Questions, evidence and activity ideas

Original examples below are not NCERT question transcriptions. The [foundations map](depth-map.md) traces prerequisites backwards from each topic; the topic labels below identify what each question probes, not a depth. Answers and possible misconceptions are included for authoring; a single response does not diagnose a learner.

| ID | Topic | Prompt | Expected answer and reasoning | Wrong answer to investigate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Conservation of mass | In a sealed vessel, 8 g of A and 12 g of B react. What is the total mass of products? | 20 g; all matter is counted. | 4 g, confusing mass difference with total. |
| Q2 | System boundary | Why can an open gas-producing reaction show a smaller final balance reading? | Escaped gas is outside the measured system. | Gas has no mass. |
| Q3 | Constant proportions | In water H:O mass is 1:8. How much oxygen combines with 4 g of hydrogen? | 32 g in water, assuming the 4 g is the hydrogen in the compound. | 8 g, failing to scale the ratio. |
| Q4 | Dalton and isotopes | Which Dalton assumption is challenged by isotopes? | All atoms of a given element having identical mass. | Atoms combine in whole-number ratios. |
| Q5 | Molecule classification | Classify O₂ and CO₂ as molecules of an element or a compound. | O₂ is an element molecule; CO₂ is a compound molecule. | Both are compounds because each has multiple atoms. |
| Q6 | Covalent bonding | What is shared in a single covalent bond? | A pair of electrons. | A proton or a whole atom. |
| Q7 | Ion charge | A neutral atom with 12 protons loses two electrons. Give its ion charge and electron count. | Mg²⁺, ten electrons; protons remain 12. | −2, confusing loss of negative charge. |
| Q8 | Reading formulae | Count each element's atoms in (NH₄)₂SO₄. | N 2, H 8, S 1, O 4 per formula unit. | H 4, not applying the outer subscript. |
| Q9 | Ionic formulae | Write the formula formed by aluminium ions Al³⁺ and oxide ions O²⁻; show charge balance. | Al₂O₃: 2(+3) + 3(−2) = 0. | Al₃O₂, swapping counts incorrectly. |
| Q10 | Ionic formulae | Give the formula for calcium hydroxide using Ca²⁺ and OH⁻. | Ca(OH)₂; two whole hydroxide ions balance +2. | CaOH₂, which does not contain two OH groups. |
| Q11 | Conductivity | Why can molten sodium chloride conduct electricity, while its solid form ordinarily cannot? | Ions can move in the melt but are fixed in the solid lattice. | Electrons move freely through both states. |
| Q12 | Molecular mass | Calculate CO₂ molecular mass using C = 12 u and O = 16 u. | 44 u per molecule. | 28 u, counting only one oxygen. |
| Q13 | Formula unit mass | Calculate Ca(OH)₂ formula unit mass using Ca = 40, O = 16 and H = 1 u. | 40 + 2(16 + 1) = 74 u. | 58 u, omitting the second hydroxide. |
| Q14 | Molecular formulae | Is it valid to replace molecular formula H₂O₂ by HO because both subscripts divide by two? | No. H₂O₂ specifies the atoms in a hydrogen peroxide molecule; HO is a different formula. | Yes, by applying ionic ratio simplification indiscriminately. |

## Practical and interactive opportunities

1. **Track mass and the system boundary** (conservation of mass): compare example readings for an open and sealed gas-producing setup; label gas, vessel and balance. Predict outcomes, then explain a discrepancy or measurement uncertainty. The book's demonstrations require teacher supervision and an appropriate safety assessment.
2. **Build fixed ratios** (constant proportions): give learners H and O tokens labelled with relative masses. Build one and several water molecules and compare atom count ratio with mass ratio. Try a different sample quantity.
3. **Electron and bond models** (bonding): move electrons between simple atoms or share pairs; show atom/ion charge separately from bond representation. Explain models and their limits; avoid implying real electrons move as little coloured balls.
4. **Formula builder** (formulae): choose ions with visible charges, balance their sum to zero, group polyatomic ions and reduce ionic ratios. Include Mg²⁺/O²⁻ and Ca²⁺/OH⁻ as checks.
5. **Property investigation** (properties): interpret a supplied table of solubility and conductivity under clearly stated solid, molten or aqueous conditions. Ask what the results support and where a generalisation needs more data.
6. **Mass calculator with explanations** (mass calculations): expand groups and show contributions step by step; name the result molecular mass or formula unit mass appropriately.

For offline use these activities need self-contained assets, accessible text alternatives, answer logic and teacher-reviewed hints. No interactive simulations or executable question pool are included here.
