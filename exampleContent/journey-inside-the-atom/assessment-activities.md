# Assessment and activity research

These prompts are **original examples**, not transcriptions of NCERT exercises. Skill IDs and depths refer to [the proposed map](depth-map.md). Answers are for teacher authoring. Use more than one independent item before deciding a learner needs remediation.

## Diagnostic and practice examples

| ID | Skill / depth | Prompt | Expected answer / reasoning | Plausible distractor and what to explore |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | R2 / D1 | What is the net relative charge of one proton and one electron? | 0, because +1 and −1 cancel. | +2: charge sign confusion. |
| Q2 | A2 / D2 | Why did observing similar cathode rays with different materials matter? | It supported electrons being common to atoms of many elements. | “Atoms have no positive charge”: does not follow. |
| Q3 | A4 / D2 | Most alpha particles crossed gold foil with little deflection. What does this suggest? | Most of the atom is space through which they pass. | Atom is solid throughout: conflicts with frequent passage. |
| Q4 | A4 / D3 | A few alpha particles deflect strongly. What can be inferred, and what cannot? | A concentrated positive region can repel them; the result alone does not prove electron paths or neutrons. | All particles hit a large solid nucleus. |
| Q5 | A6 / D2 | In Bohr's model, what happens when an electron moves to a higher energy level? | It absorbs the energy difference. | It releases energy: reversed transition. |
| Q6 | P5 / D3 | Neutral atom X has Z = 8 and A = 18. Find p, n and e. | 8 protons, 10 neutrons, 8 electrons. | 18 neutrons: confuses total and part. |
| Q7 | P2 / D3 | An atom has 11 protons and 10 electrons. Is it neutral? | No; net charge +1. It is an ion. | Neutral because electrons are almost equal. |
| Q8 | E1, E2 / D3 | Give the shell arrangement and valence electron count of neutral sulfur, Z = 16. | 2,8,6; six valence electrons. | 16 valence electrons: total versus outer-shell confusion. |
| Q9 | E3 / D2 | Compare common valency of sodium and neon. | Sodium 1, neon 0; their outer configurations differ. | Both 1 because each is an element. |
| Q10 | I1, I2 / D3 | Pair A: ¹²₆C and ¹⁴₆C. Pair B: ⁴⁰₁₈Ar and ⁴⁰₂₀Ca. Classify. | A isotopes: same Z; B isobars: same A, different Z. | Switching terms: compare proton and nucleon counts explicitly. |
| Q11 | I3 / D3 | Isotope masses 10 u and 12 u occur at 25% and 75%. Find approximate average. | 0.25 × 10 + 0.75 × 12 = 11.5 u. | 11 u: equal-weight average despite unequal abundance. |
| Q12 | A7 / D4 | A diagram shows electrons on circular lines. Does it prove they follow those paths? | No; the diagram represents a model; modern theory uses probability distributions. | “Yes, the diagram is a photograph.” |

## Activities and representations

1. **Scattering as inference (A4):** Use a pre-drawn set of simulated alpha paths around an unseen target. Count straight, deflected and returning paths, propose a hidden structure, and explain what cannot be concluded. A digital simulation should be labelled as a model; geometry and probabilities are not to scale. The textbook points to [PhET Rutherford Scattering](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/rutherford-scattering); verify licences and offline availability before packaging.
2. **Build an atom (P1–P5):** Use labelled counters for protons, neutrons and electrons. Change one count at a time and ask what changes: identity, isotope, or charge. Record Z, A and net charge. No physical props required if a local interactive equivalent is supplied.
3. **Shell and valency cards (E1–E3):** Build shell diagrams for the first 18 elements; connect the outer count to familiar combining examples. Label models as schematic and discuss carbon sharing and helium's two-electron shell.
4. **Isotope average (I1, I3):** Four tokens represent three atoms of mass approximately 35 u and one of 37 u; calculate the mean and vary the mixture. Discuss why each individual mass number is still an integer. NCERT also points to [PhET Isotopes and Atomic Mass](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/isotopes-and-atomic-mass).
5. **Model timeline (A1–A7):** Learners order historical proposals, evidence and limitations, then write how one new observation changed a model. Include early Indian ideas without equating them to modern experiments.

## Textbook exercise and asset inventory

The official source has in-section “Pause and Ponder” prompts, **15 numbered end-of-chapter review questions** on printed pages 158–160, and extension tasks on page 161. These cover model evidence and chronology, attraction and stability, identifying isotopes/isobars, particle calculations, electron distributions and valency, completing a table, and a hypothetical change in electron mass. The source also suggests role-play, a prediction board, research on applications, simulations, a documentary report and shell-count graphs. See the [PDF](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/iesc108.pdf) for exact wording, diagrams and all subparts. The question count excludes the embedded prompts and extension tasks.

Important visual references include the cathode-ray tube, Thomson model, gold-foil setup and results, Rutherford/Bohr models, shell distributions, isotopes, and a model timeline. Any recreated visual needs an accessible text equivalent, scientific review, and a clear indication that diagram sizes are schematic. Source illustrations are not included in this package.
