# Structure of the Atom

Atoms are not indivisible. Every atom is built from three **sub-atomic particles**: the
**electron** (negative charge), the **proton** (positive charge), and the **neutron** (no
charge). Protons and neutrons sit in the **nucleus**; electrons move around it. The electron
was discovered by **J. J. Thomson**, the proton came from **Goldstein's** canal-ray
experiments, and the **neutron** was discovered by **James Chadwick** (its mass is nearly
equal to a proton's).

## How our picture of the atom developed

- **Thomson's model.** The atom is a sphere of positive charge with electrons embedded in it,
  like seeds in a watermelon. Positive and negative charges are equal, so the atom is neutral.
- **Rutherford's model.** In the gold-foil experiment, most alpha particles passed straight
  through, a few deflected, and very few bounced back. So the atom is **mostly empty space**
  with a small, dense, positively charged **nucleus** that holds nearly all its mass; electrons
  revolve around it.
- **Bohr's model.** Electrons revolve only in certain fixed **energy shells** (K, L, M, N…)
  without losing energy, which makes the atom stable.

> [!tip]
> A revolving electron in Rutherford's model should lose energy and fall into the nucleus.
> Bohr fixed this by allowing only special, stable shells.

## Electrons in shells

The maximum number of electrons a shell can hold is **2n²** (K = 2, L = 8, M = 18, N = 32),
and the outermost shell holds at most 8. Inner shells fill before outer ones.

The electrons in the outermost shell are **valence electrons**, and they decide an atom's
**valency** — its combining capacity. An atom with only a few outer electrons tends to **lose**
them; one with a nearly full outer shell tends to **gain** electrons. An atom whose outermost
shell is already full (8 electrons, or 2 for the first shell) is stable and has valency
**zero** — these are the **noble gases**.

## Counting the particles

- **Atomic number (Z)** = number of protons. It defines the element.
- **Mass number (A)** = protons + neutrons.
- In a neutral atom, electrons = protons.

## Isotopes and isobars

- **Isotopes**: same element (same Z), different mass numbers — e.g. carbon-12 and carbon-14.
- **Isobars**: different elements (different Z), same mass number — e.g. calcium-40 and
  argon-40.

Isotopes of an element have the **same chemical properties**. Some are useful: an isotope of
**uranium** fuels nuclear reactors, an isotope of **cobalt** treats cancer, and an isotope of
**iodine** treats goitre.
