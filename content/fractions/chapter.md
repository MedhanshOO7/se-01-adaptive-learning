# Equivalent fractions

Two fractions are **equivalent** when they describe the same amount, even though
they are written with different numbers. For example, `1/2` and `2/4` look
different but cover exactly the same part of a whole.

## Why they are equal

A fraction splits a whole into **equal parts**. The bottom number (the
*denominator*) says how many equal parts there are. The top number (the
*numerator*) says how many of them we take.

If you **multiply the numerator and the denominator by the same number**, you are
just cutting each part into smaller equal pieces — the amount shaded does not
change.

> [!tip]
> Multiplying `1/2` by `2/2` gives `2/4`. You multiplied by 1 in disguise, so the
> value stays the same.

## Worked example

Is `2/3` equivalent to `4/6`?

Multiply the top and bottom of `2/3` by 2: `2×2 = 4` and `3×2 = 6`. So
`2/3 = 4/6`. They are equivalent.

## Simplest form

Going the other way, you can **divide** the numerator and denominator by the same
number to reach the **simplest form**. `4/8` divides by 4 to become `1/2`, which
cannot be reduced any further.
