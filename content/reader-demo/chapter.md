# Fractions: parts of a whole

Imagine you and a friend are sharing an apple. You cut it into two equal pieces and each take one. You have **one half** of the apple.

Fractions help us describe amounts like this: a part of something whole.

## Reading a fraction

In **3/4**, the number below the line tells us that the whole is divided into four equal parts. The number above the line tells us that we have three of those parts.

| Fraction | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| 1/4 | One of four equal parts |
| 2/4 | Two of four equal parts |
| 3/4 | Three of four equal parts |

The pieces must be equal. If one piece is larger than another, counting them does not tell us the fraction of the whole.

## Different names, same amount

Cut the same apple into four equal pieces instead. If you take two pieces, you still have half the apple. We can write this as **1/2 = 2/4**.

These are called *equivalent fractions*. Multiplying the top and bottom numbers by the same number gives another name for the same amount.

> Think of it this way: cutting each piece in two creates more pieces, but it does not create more apple.

## Comparing and combining

When the pieces are the same size, compare how many you have. Three quarters is more than one quarter. And two halves together make one whole.
