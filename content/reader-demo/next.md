# Adding fractions

You already know that fractions describe equal pieces. Now let’s use that idea to put some of those pieces together.

## Start with the same-sized pieces

Imagine a tray cut into four equal pieces. You take one piece, and a friend takes two. Together, you have three of the four pieces:

**1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4**

Because each piece is a quarter, you can count the pieces directly. The bottom number stays at 4; the top numbers add up to 3.

| You have | Your friend has | Together |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/4 | 2/4 | 3/4 |
| 1/5 | 2/5 | 3/5 |
| 2/6 | 3/6 | 5/6 |

## When the pieces look different

What about **1/2 + 1/4**? A half and a quarter are different-sized pieces, so first give them the same name. One half is equal to two quarters:

**1/2 + 1/4 = 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4**

> Think of the denominator as the size of the pieces. Make the pieces the same size before counting how many you have.

## Try thinking it through

You have 1/3 of a cake and someone gives you another 1/3. How much do you have now? There are two pieces, each one third of the whole: **2/3**.
